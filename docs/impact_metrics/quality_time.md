# Quality Time

## Definition

A composite score representing the relational value generated by sharing interactions. One Quality Time minute approximates one minute of quality in-person social contact. The metric captures what sharing *creates* (relationships, trust, belonging) rather than what it *saves* (money, carbon, time).

Quality Time is analogous to a carbon footprint: just as carbon footprinting normalizes diverse emission types into CO2-equivalent, Quality Time normalizes diverse interaction types -- a dinner party, a borrowed lawnmower, a ride to the airport -- into a single directional score. The score is informed by social psychology research but involves modeling assumptions; it is directional, not clinically precise.

## Formula

```
QT = duration * modality * group_size * tie_strength * reciprocity * novelty * vulnerability
```

**Subsidiary metrics** (computed at aggregation time, display-only):

```
belonging_minutes = duration * modality         # co-presence quality
trust_credits     = vulnerability * reciprocity  # depth of trust exercised
```

| Reciprocity | 0.0 | Deterministic (no uncertainty) |

Composite uncertainty uses quadrature on relative stddevs: `sigma_rel^2 = sum((sigma_factor / factor)^2)`. Typical composite relative uncertainty is ~50% for a gear handoff, ~56% for an experience with group variance.

## Methodology (User-Facing)

### Summary

Quality Time measures the relational value of sharing interactions. One QT minute approximates one minute of quality in-person social contact.

### How It Works

Each sharing interaction is scored across seven attributes:
1. **Duration** -- how long the interaction lasts
2. **Modality** -- in-person contact scores highest; text-only scores lowest
3. **Group size** -- small groups (3-5) produce the highest per-person wellbeing
4. **Tie strength** -- closer relationships amplify relational value
5. **Reciprocity** -- giving produces equal or greater wellbeing than receiving
6. **Novelty** -- first-time interactions score higher than routine ones
7. **Vulnerability** -- sharing in contexts that require trust (e.g., hosting) amplifies connection

The attributes are multiplied together to produce a composite score anchored to real minutes: a 60-minute in-person shared activity between moderately close people scores roughly 60 QT.

### Sources

Social psychology research provides directional support for each attribute weight. No single study validates the composite formula; the weights are modeling decisions informed by the literature.

### Limitations

- The composite formula multiplies seven factors, compounding modeling assumptions.
- Duration defaults are rough averages; actual interaction times vary.
- Vulnerability and modality are currently assigned by transaction type, not observed.
- The score is directional and motivational, not a clinical measure of social wellbeing.

## Assumptions

1. **Linear duration scaling**: Affective quality of a social episode scales linearly with duration. Research support: Directional (Kahneman et al. 2004).
2. **Multiplicative attribute model**: Attributes combine multiplicatively, meaning low scores on any dimension substantially reduce the total. Research support: Conceptual (no study validates this specific composition).
3. **In-person superiority**: Face-to-face interaction produces meaningfully more social benefit than digital. Research support: Direct (Cacioppo & Cacioppo 2014; Dunbar 2016).
4. **Small group optimum**: Groups of 3-5 produce the highest per-person wellbeing. Research support: Directional (Dunbar 1992, 2010; Baumeister & Leary 1995).
5. **Giving >= receiving**: The act of giving produces equal or greater wellbeing than receiving. Research support: Direct (Dunn, Aknin & Norton 2008; Post 2005).
6. **Novelty premium**: First-time interactions are more impactful than routine ones. Research support: Directional (Aron et al. 2000; Reis et al. 2000).
7. **Sufficiency threshold**: ~6 hours of weekly social contact is the diminishing-returns threshold. Research support: Directional (Helliwell & Huang 2013; ATUS data).

## Per-Attribute Provenance and Overrides

Each of the seven QT attributes (`estimated_duration_minutes`, `modality`,
`group_size`, `tie_strength`, `reciprocity`, `novelty`, `vulnerability`)
carries its own `Provenance` field. The completion-time draft RPC stamps
`source = LLM` for AI-inferred attributes, `source = FORMULA` for
graph-derived ones (e.g., tie strength from prior-interaction count), and
`source = CONFIG_DEFAULT` when no signal is available. When the user
overrides an attribute through the completion modal, that single attribute's
provenance flips to `source = USER`; sibling attributes keep their original
source. Refresh-gating respects this signal: `ShouldRefresh()` skips any
attribute whose `Provenance.source` is `USER`, so user overrides survive
algorithm-version bumps.

The composite `quality_time_minutes` is always server-computed from the
per-attribute inputs and is never directly user-settable.

## Future Work

- **Effort asymmetry**: Scale per-person QT toward disproportionate
  contributors when `PlanningContribution` records show uneven hosting effort.
- **Multi-host vulnerability averaging**: Average vulnerability across hosts
  when contributions show distributed hosting.
- **Follow-through signal**: Bump tie strength / novelty for the next event
  when post-completion chat continues for more than 7 days.
- **Cross-community bridging bonus**: Apply a small tie-strength multiplier
  when attendees come from multiple communities.
- **Calibration study**: Compare QT scores against self-reported interaction
  quality to validate attribute weights.
- **Baseline and growth targets**: Track community-level QT trends over time.
