Event: Mother's Day Brunch

Start with a sentence

Leslie, the host

Leslie types one sentence, the way she'd text a friend. Ripls picks out the date and time, and reads "our place" as her home address. She adds a photo, saves, and shares it three ways: a link for a text thread, two friends invited by name, and her family circle added as a group.

Join from a text

Susan, an invitee

Susan opens the link from a text and lands on the event itself — the photo, the time, the place, and who's already coming. She confirms with her phone number and a six-digit code. There's nothing to install and no password to pick.

Plan in context

Leslie, the host

Leslie is notified as RSVPs come in. She lists what the day still needs — brunch dishes, dessert, craft supplies — on the event itself, next to the list of who's coming, so every guest sees it without her repeating it in a group text.

Build momentum

Leslie, the host

Each event has its own thread, with the people who are coming. Leslie floats her crown idea there rather than in the family group text, and the replies come back in the same place, without reaching anyone who isn't involved.

Claim what you'll bring

Lisa, an invitee

Lisa taps "Brunch dishes" and adds a note about what the kids are making. The list updates for everyone: dishes are covered, dessert is still open. Nobody has to ask who's bringing what.

Watch the day happen

Bryan, at the brunch

Photos land in the event thread straight from the kitchen — kids in aprons, the cake, the crowns in action. They collect where the day was planned rather than scattering across everyone's camera rolls.

Wrap up

Leslie, the host

Afterward, Leslie wraps the brunch up. Everyone who said yes is ticked already, so confirming who came is a glance rather than a form, and Ripls totals the time the family spent together — about four hours. The photos, the plan and the thread all stay on the event.
